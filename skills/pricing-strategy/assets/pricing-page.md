# Pricing Page Template

Copy into your site's pricing page and fill in the blanks. The structure below
is the default — adapt the wording to your voice, but keep the tier shape and
the objection FAQ. The rule for the whole page: a visitor should be able to
answer "what does it cost, what do I get, and is it for me?" in about 20
seconds.

## Tiers

Use two or three. The middle tier is the one you designed for your ICP; make it
visibly the default. Every tier is described by its **value-metric allowance**,
not by vague adjectives.

### [Starter] — $[x] / [mo]
*For [the narrow use case / the hesitant first buyer].*
- [Value-metric allowance, e.g. 3 projects]
- [The one capability that gets them to the "aha"]
- [The limit that nudges them up when they outgrow it]

### [Pro] — $[y] / [mo]  ← most popular
*For [your main ICP].*
- [Value-metric allowance, e.g. 25 projects]
- [Everything in Starter]
- [The capability that makes this the obvious choice]
- [The collaboration / export / integration that pays for itself]

### [Scale] — $[z] / [mo]
*For [buyers whose value scales faster, or teams].*
- [Value-metric allowance, e.g. unlimited projects]
- [Everything in Pro]
- [The thing only bigger buyers need — SSO, priority support, audit log]
- [A reason it isn't just "Pro with a bigger number"]

**Footer line:** Prices in [currency]. Taxes handled at checkout. Annual billing
available ([N months free](#annual)) — [switch button].

> Keep tier count low. A fourth or fifth tier usually means you haven't decided
> which buyer you serve. If a feature fits no tier cleanly, cut it from the
> page rather than silently bundling it.

## Objection-handling FAQ

Answer the four objections that block a purchase. Rewrite the answers to your
product, but keep the questions — they are the ones real buyers ask.

**Is [product] worth the price?**
> Tie the price to the outcome and the alternative. "It replaces [what they pay
> now, in money or hours]. If it saves you [X hours / $Y] a month, it pays for
> itself." Give a number, not an adjective.

**What if it isn't for me?**
> Remove the risk: a free tier, a trial, or a money-back window. Say plainly how
> to leave, and confirm no dark patterns. "Cancel in one click; export your data
> anytime."

**Can I cancel or change plans later?**
> Yes — state the mechanics (upgrade/downgrade, proration, how cancellation
> works). Buyers who know the exit is easy are more likely to enter.

**Why shouldn't I pick the cheaper tier?**
> Name the limit honestly and describe the moment they'll outgrow it. Do not
> shame the cheap tier; a happy small customer grows. "Starter is perfect until
> you run more than [n] [metric] — then Pro is cheaper than the workaround."

**Do I have to pay for the whole team / all my usage?**
> (Include only if relevant.) Explain the metric: what counts, what's included,
> and how to predict the bill. Unpredictable limits are a churn source.

**How does billing and tax work?**
> Say who processes payment and that taxes are handled at checkout. Do not
> promise tax outcomes you have not confirmed with an accountant.
