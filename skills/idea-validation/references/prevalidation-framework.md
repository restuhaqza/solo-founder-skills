## 1. Why pre-validate

Most ideas should die cheaply. Spending two hours to disqualify a bad idea is a win; spending six months building one is the disaster this ladder exists to prevent. Move down the ladder only when the rung above it passed — never skip ahead because the idea feels exciting.

## 2. Phase 2 — the two-hour desk rubric

Do this alone, at a desk, before talking to anyone. Answer with a number where you can.

- **Do I have this pain myself, or unusual access to the people who do?** (Best predictor of survival.)
- **Where did this idea come from?** Your own workflow and niche communities age well; ideas lifted from social-media threads and AI idea-generators tend to fall apart at research.
- **How frequent is the pain?** Daily/weekly beats "a few times a year."
- **Is there proof anyone pays for a fix today?** Look for revenue-generating incumbents or paid workarounds, not just chatter.
- **Can I name 20 real people I could interview this month?**

If you cannot answer the last two with specifics, stop here. Do not proceed on vibes.

## 3. Phase 20 — the twenty-hour market and problem check

Spend roughly twenty hours building evidence *without building the product*.

- **Demand signal.** Google Trends, Reddit/forum/Slack search, "how do I…" queries. Are people actively describing this problem in their own words?
- **Money signal.** Who already pays, and how much? Map 3–10 incumbents and adjacent tools with their prices.
- **Reachability.** Where exactly does this segment gather, and can you get in without spamming? (If the only channel is cold DMs, the acquisition cost math is harder.)
- **Workaround signal.** What hacky thing do people do today? A painful spreadsheet or a WhatsApp group is a strong signal.
- **A cheap landing-page test.** A single page describing the outcome, with a real call to action (a deposit beats an email). Run it for a week.

## 4. Phase 200 — the customer-validation phase

Only now do you spend real hours talking to customers and testing payment. This is where Step 3–5 of the skill live: Mom Test interviews, competitor battle card, and a willingness-to-pay test. If Phase 20 already raised a red flag, you have saved yourself this entire phase.

## 5. Red-flag checklist

Any *one* of these is usually a stop signal:

- [ ] The only evidence is positive sentiment ("everyone I asked loved it").
- [ ] Nobody is currently paying to solve the problem in any form.
- [ ] You cannot name where the buyers gather.
- [ ] The problem is real but infrequent (a few times a year).
- [ ] The buyer and the user are different people with conflicting incentives and you have access to only one.
- [ ] Your main reason to build is "it would be fun" or "I saw it trending."
- [ ] The idea depends on a platform or API that could cut you off.
- [ ] You would need to educate the market that the problem exists at all.

## 6. Scoring the ladder

Use this as a gut-check before Step 6 of the main workflow:

| Rung | Pass looks like | Fail action |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 2h | You know 20+ reachable people with the pain | Rework the idea or shelve it |
| 20h | Demand, money, and reachability signals all present | Fix the weakest signal, or shelve |
| 200h | Real commitments (deposits / paid pilots) from a narrow segment | Pivot the assumption, or kill |

A failed rung is not a failed founder. It is the ladder doing its job.
