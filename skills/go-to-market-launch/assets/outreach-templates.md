# Outreach Templates

Patterns for cold and warm outreach. **The agent researches the target and drafts the message; the founder personalizes and sends every one.** Bulk or automated sending burns the domain, the account, and the relationship — the whole point is that it is one person, writing to one person.

## How to use these

1. The agent researches each target: who they are, what they've said publicly, what they need.
2. The agent maps a *stated* need to a *verified* capability of the product. No unverified claims.
3. The agent drafts a short message from a template below.
4. **The founder edits it into their own voice and sends it.** If it doesn't sound like them, it won't work.

Rules that keep this from becoming spam:
- One message, one person, one specific reason you're writing **to them**.
- Lead with their problem, not your product.
- No attachments on the first message; no walls of text; no hype adjectives.
- Personalize the first line so it could not have been sent to anyone else.
- If there's no genuine reason to reach out, don't.

---

## 1. Warm intro from a community interaction

> Hi <name> — saw your post in <community> about <specific problem>. I've been working on <product>, which does <one concrete thing> for <their kind of work>. Not pitching; if it's useful I'm happy to set it up with you directly, and if it isn't, your take on <specific question> would still help me.

## 2. Cold, researched, specific (the hard one)

> Hi <name>,
>
> <One sentence proving you read their work: a post, a talk, a product decision.>
> You mentioned <their stated problem>. I built <product> to handle exactly that — <one concrete outcome, no adjectives>.
>
> Would it be useful if I showed you how it'd fit <their workflow>? If not, no worries at all — I'd just value your read on whether <specific question>.
>
> <name>
> <link with UTM>

## 3. Newsletter / podcast / curator pitch

> Hi <name> — I read <their newsletter/episode> regularly, especially <specific piece>.
>
> I have a story that fits your <audience/format>: <one-line angle — a number, a lesson, a contrarian finding>. Not a product pitch — <what the reader/listener walks away with>.
>
> Happy to send a draft in your format if it's a fit. Either way, <genuine comment on their work>.

## 4. Re-engage a signup who never activated

> Hi <name> — you signed up for <product> a while back but I don't think you got to <the aha moment>. That's on me, not you.
>
> Want me to set it up with you for 15 minutes so you can see if it's useful? If it's not for you, just say so and I'll close the loop.

## 5. Follow-up (the one most people forget)

> Hi <name> — following up once on this, in case it got buried. No pressure; if now isn't the time, a quick "not now" is genuinely helpful and I'll leave it.

Send **one** follow-up. Two is persistence; three is harassment.

---

## Before you hit send (checklist)

- [ ] Could this message only have been sent to this person? (If not, rewrite the first line.)
- [ ] Does it lead with *their* problem, not your product?
- [ ] Is every claim verifiable? No invented numbers or features.
- [ ] Is it short enough to read on a phone without scrolling twice?
- [ ] Would you be comfortable if they posted it publicly?
- [ ] Does it sound like you, in your own voice?

## Boundary

The founder sends every message, from their own account, in their own name. The agent drafts and researches only. Do not set up automated sending, and do not send the same template to a list.
