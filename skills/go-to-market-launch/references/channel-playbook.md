# The Channel Playbook

Which distribution channel to go deep on, what it realistically returns, and how not to get burned in each one.

## 1. Pick one, go deep

The temptation is to be everywhere on day one. Resist it. Five half-efforts give you five channels you cannot read, cannot improve, and cannot attribute — and duplicate copy across communities reads as spam. Choose the one place where your *exact* buyer already gathers and where you can show up weekly for at least 60 days, then earn the right to add a second channel.

A useful filter: **can you be useful there without pitching?** The channels that convert (see §4) are the ones where you contribute first and mention your product occasionally. If the only way you can participate is by pitching, it is the wrong channel.

## 2. Channel table — effort, expectation, etiquette

Expectations below are *directional*, not guarantees. Your numbers will depend on the niche, the offer, and how well you fit the room. Treat "realistic expectation" as the shape of the outcome (a spike, a slow trickle, a compounding curve), not a promise.

| Channel | Effort | Realistic expectation | Etiquette |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Product Hunt** | High prep, one day of intense presence | A one-day spike in visits; a small fraction convert; ranking is noisy and partly luck | Land on a **Thursday**; prepare the listing and gallery in advance; reply to every comment the day of; ask supporters to *use it*, not just upvote |
| **Show HN (Hacker News)** | Medium writing, high presence on the day | A spike if the story is genuinely interesting; brutal if it reads as marketing; can send sustained technical users | Post only when it stands on its own merits; plain honest title with no marketing voice; disclose you built it; be in the comments answering hard questions first — see §3 |
| **Reddit subreddits** | Medium; requires reading each sub's rules | Modest, steady, sometimes surprisingly good in a *niche* sub; near-zero in a general one | Read the rules and pinned posts first; participate for weeks before you ever mention your product; share the story/method, not a link dump; no cross-posting the same text |
| **Niche Slack / Discord** | Low once you are in; ongoing presence | Highest conversion of any channel, because the room is exactly your buyer | Answer questions with no pitch attached; ask the mods before promoting; never DM the whole member list |
| **X (Twitter)** | Continuous, compounding | Slow build unless you already have an audience; good for build-in-public and for DMs that lead somewhere | Be specific and useful; thread the *lesson*, not the launch; engage others before broadcasting |
| **LinkedIn** | Medium, steady | Good for B2B and services; weak for consumer/dev tools | Write for a professional reader; no engagement-bait; connect before you pitch |
| **YouTube** | High production effort | Compounds over months; strong for how-to and for AI-search citations | Answer one clear question per video; don't lead with a hard sell; keep titles honest |
| **Newsletters** | Low to write, hard to get listed | A placement in a relevant newsletter can outperform a week of posting — if it is on-topic | Pitch the curator with a specific angle; respect their format and length; don't send the same pitch to ten at once |
| **SEO / content** | Slow, high upfront | Compounds for years; the pillar page earns traffic while you sleep | Answer the real query; don't build thin pages for every keyword permutation — see `content-and-geo.md` |

## 3. Hacker News etiquette in detail

HN is the channel that punishes marketing voice hardest and rewards honesty hardest. The rules that keep you out of trouble:

- **Post it only if it is interesting on its own merits.** HN wants novel tools, deep write-ups, and hard-won lessons — not "check out my product."
- **Write the title yourself, plainly.** No "🚀", no "the best", no clickbait. If it is a tool, "Show HN: <plain description>" is the convention.
- **Be transparent that you built it.** The community assumes a Show HN is self-promotion; pretending otherwise is what gets flagged.
- **Answer the technical questions before the commercial ones.** The comment thread *is* the content. Reply to skeptics with substance, not defensiveness.
- **Do not ask for upvotes, ever.** Vote-ring behavior is detected and buried, and it can get the account restricted.
- **Take the whole day off.** Momentum in the first few hours decides the outcome; a post you abandon is a post that dies.

The upside is real: a well-received HN post can drive a burst of savvy technical users and lasting SEO. The downside is equally real: a hard pitch gets flagged, buried, and remembered.

## 4. Community vs cold conversion

Industry reporting keeps pointing at the same shape: conversion from **targeted communities** (a niche Slack or Discord where the whole room is your buyer) runs dramatically higher — often cited around **~18%** — than conversion from **cold, untargeted traffic** at roughly **~2%**. Treat those figures as directional, not exact; the point is the order of magnitude. Being a known, useful presence in the right room beats shouting into an anonymous feed by a wide margin.

The practical consequence: spend your scarce hours where you can be **relevant**, not merely **visible**. A personalized answer to one real question in a niche Discord outperforms a link posted to a general subreddit, and it does not burn your reputation.

## 5. Launch is a spike — the "keep showing up" rule

Every launch produces a curve that goes up fast and down fast. That is normal. The founders who win are not the ones with the biggest launch day; they are the ones who are still posting useful things on day 30, day 100, day 365. Plan the follow-through with the same care as the launch itself: after the spike, you want a reason for people to keep arriving — a content engine, an engineering-as-marketing artifact, a steady presence on the one channel you chose.

Concretely, the week *after* launch matters more than the week before:
- Publish a specific build-in-public post about what the launch actually did (the real numbers, the surprises).
- Answer every unanswered question you saw in the comments, even late.
- Onboard the stragglers who signed up but never activated.

## 6. A 60-day channel experiment

Give each channel a fair, bounded test so you can cut without guilt and double down without hand-waving.

- **Name the outcome up front.** Not "post more" but "10 qualified replies" or "25 activated signups."
- **Commit the hours.** e.g. three hours a week for 60 days, protected on the calendar.
- **Post consistently, in the room's native style.** Rewrite per community; never paste the same copy twice.
- **Review at day 60 against the outcome.** Hit it → double down and consider a second channel. Miss it → stop, and move the same hours to the next candidate. Document the decision either way so you don't relitigate it.
